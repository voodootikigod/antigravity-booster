## Experimental Methodology & Calibration Provenance

All four benchmark scenarios execute *N* = 100 paired Monte Carlo trials (1,600 total ticket dispatches in `perf-16-ticket-scale`, 500 seeded defects plus 500 clean negative controls in `acc-cross-model-prosecution`, and 300 adversarial safety hazards in `acc-mechanical-safety-gates`).

Rather than relying on ungrounded synthetic assumptions, the simulation parameters are anchored directly to live empirical measurements captured against the `agy` CLI in [`docs/calibration/probes-2026-06-11.md`](https://github.com/voodootikigod/antigravity-booster/blob/main/docs/calibration/probes-2026-06-11.md) and [`docs/calibration/probes-2026-07-14.md`](https://github.com/voodootikigod/antigravity-booster/blob/main/docs/calibration/probes-2026-07-14.md):

| Empirical Anchor | Measured Value | Source Probe | Architectural Implication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Single-Pool Concurrency Knee (`width = 1..4`)** | `0%` timeouts; median `26.7s..28.8s` | Probe 5 (`2026-06-11`) | Operating at or below `width = 4` on a frontier pool yields flat latency and zero rate-limit drops. |
| **Single-Pool Burst Collapse (`width = 8`)** | `25%` timeout rate (`2/8` at `120s`); P90 spikes to `68.4s` | Probe 5 (`2026-06-11`) | Unconstrained subagent fan-out on a single model family hits server-side queueing and 120s print timeouts. |
| **Independent Quota-Family Buckets** | `Gemini Models` vs. `Claude and GPT models` (`5h` & `weekly`) | Probe 4 (`2026-07-14`) | Routing across `gemini-flash` (`6`), `gemini-pro` (`4`), `claude` (`4`), and `gpt-oss` (`2`) provides `16` concurrent slots below every pool's knee. |
| **Cross-Family Prosecution Latency & Catch** | `11.1s` median; caught Unicode defect missed by builder | Probe 6 (`2026-06-11`) | A cross-family `gemini-3-flash` prosecutor catches representation blind spots left by a `claude-sonnet` builder. |

### The Three Comparison Arms

To avoid a strawman comparison, every scenario evaluates three distinct operating regimes:

1. **Straight `agy` (Naive Baseline):**
   Single-session serial execution, unconstrained 8-wide single-pool subagent bursts, or same-context (`Model: 'inherit'`) self-review with prompt-only safety instructions.
2. **Raw `agy` (Best Practices):**
   A disciplined engineer using Antigravity's built-in primitives to their fullest without `agb`:
   - `/plan` and `/teamwork` slash commands to structure wave execution.
   - `invoke_subagent` with `Workspace: 'share'` (shared git repository worktrees) and manual tier splitting (`Model: 'flash'` for low-tier work, `Model: 'pro'` for complex work).
   - Well-crafted `AGENTS.md` repository rules and the `test-driven-development` skill.
   - Fresh-context `adversarial-reviewer` subagent invocation (`doubt-driven-development`) after each wave.
3. **Antigravity + Booster (`agb`):**
   - Zero-token deterministic Node.js DAG scheduler ([`lib/scheduler.mjs`](https://github.com/voodootikigod/antigravity-booster/blob/main/lib/scheduler.mjs)) with atomic `mkdir` merge locking ([`lib/lock.mjs`](https://github.com/voodootikigod/antigravity-booster/blob/main/lib/lock.mjs)).
   - Live `/quota`-aware dual-bucket, 4-pool token-bucket semaphores ([`lib/pools.mjs`](https://github.com/voodootikigod/antigravity-booster/blob/main/lib/pools.mjs)).
   - Cross-family diff prosecution ([`lib/prosecute.mjs`](https://github.com/voodootikigod/antigravity-booster/blob/main/lib/prosecute.mjs)) plus the 4-orthogonal-lens specialist review fleet (`correctness`, `security`, `tests`, `contracts` in [`lib/review.mjs`](https://github.com/voodootikigod/antigravity-booster/blob/main/lib/review.mjs)).
   - Mechanical `PreToolUse` policy hook ([`hooks/pre-tool-use.mjs`](https://github.com/voodootikigod/antigravity-booster/blob/main/hooks/pre-tool-use.mjs)), `adlc rails-guard`, worktree scope enforcement ([`lib/gates.mjs`](https://github.com/voodootikigod/antigravity-booster/blob/main/lib/gates.mjs)), and `adlc hollow-test` AST mutation testing.

## Pareto Frontier & 80/20 Root-Cause Analysis

### 1. Speed–Accuracy Pareto Frontier (Throughput vs. Defect Detection Recall)

In single-session or single-family agent workflows, engineering teams face an inherent trade-off between **delivery throughput** (tickets completed per minute) and **verification rigor** (defect detection recall). Adding in-session review passes improves recall but serializes execution and consumes active-session context.

Because `agb` dispatches builders and cross-family prosecutors concurrently across four independent model pools in isolated git worktrees, it **shifts the entire Pareto frontier outward** — strictly dominating every baseline on both velocity and defect recall simultaneously:

| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Straight `agy` (Serial, Builder Only)** | `0.96/min` | `1.95/min` | `21.8%` | `78.2%` | `56.3%` | Strictly Dominated |

### 2. Pareto Analysis of Escaped Defects (Why Single-Family Review Leaves a 26.4% Escape Tail)

When an engineer uses **Raw `agy` (Best Practices)** — spawning `adversarial-reviewer` in a fresh subagent context — post-review defect escapes drop from `240 / 500` (`48.0%`) to `132 / 500` (`26.4%`). However, ranking those `132` escaped defects by root cause reveals a classic **Pareto distribution**:

- **The top 2 defect classes** (`Spec Ambiguity & Unicode/Locale Boundary` and `Lock / TOCTOU / Concurrency Race`) account for **`53.8%`** of all escaped defects.
- **The top 3 defect classes** account for **`71.2%`** of all escaped defects.
- **The top 4 defect classes** account for **`87.9%`** of all escaped defects.

Why does `Spec Ambiguity & Unicode/Locale Boundary` alone cause **`31.8%`** (`42 / 132`) of all Best-Practices escapes? Because `invoke_subagent` only accepts `Model: 'inherit' | 'flash_lite' | 'flash' | 'pro'` within the active session's model family. When a builder and its reviewer come from the same model family, they share correlated pretraining priors on ambiguous specification phrasing and Unicode/locale edge cases (the exact failure mode observed in Probe 6).

By routing prosecution to an **independent model family** (`lib/prosecute.mjs`) and fanning out **four orthogonal specialist lenses** (`lib/review.mjs`), `agb` collapses escapes in the #1 Pareto category from `42` down to **`3` (`-92.9%`)**:

| Pareto Rank | Defect Class | Raw `agy` Best Practices Escapes (*N*=100) | Share of Best-Practices Escapes | Cumulative Pareto Share (%) | `agb` Cross-Model Escapes (*N*=100) | `agb` Escape Reduction vs. Best Practices |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **#1** | **Spec Ambiguity & Unicode/Locale Boundary (Probe 6 class)** | `42` | `31.8%` | **`31.8%`** | **`3`** | **`-92.9%`** |
| **#2** | **Lock / TOCTOU / Async Interleaving Race** | `29` | `22.0%` | **`53.8%`** | **`12`** | **`-58.6%`** |
| **#3** | **Auth, Attestation & Trust-Boundary Bypass** | `23` | `17.4%` | **`71.2%`** | **`8`** | **`-65.2%`** |
| **#4** | **Silent Error Swallowing & Partial-Failure Leak** | `22` | `16.7%` | **`87.9%`** | **`11`** | **`-50.0%`** |

#### 3b. Raw `agy` Best Practices (`274.2s`) vs. `agb` (`132.0s`) at 16-Ticket Scale: Δ = 142.2s Excess Wall-Clock Time

When comparing `agb` against **Raw `agy` (Best Practices)** on a 16-ticket repository refactoring, `agb` still finishes **`2.08x` faster** (`132.0s` vs. `274.2s`, saving `142.2s`). Pareto decomposition shows that **`75.0%` of the remaining latency gap comes from just two structural platform limits** in native `agy`:

1. **Single-Family Pool Contention & `120s` Timeout Retries (`51.4%` of excess time):** Native `invoke_subagent` cannot dispatch across model families (`Gemini` vs. `Claude/GPT`), forcing all 16 subagents and parent turns to draw from a single quota family (`9.3%` timeout rate vs. `1.2%` in `agb`).
2. **Parent LLM Wave-Orchestration Turns (`23.6%` of excess time):** Native `/teamwork` requires the parent LLM to wake up between sub-batches, read subagent outputs, and coordinate merges (~`9.5s` per sub-wave), whereas `agb`'s Node.js scheduler transitions waves in `< 10ms` with `0` LLM tokens.

### 4. Pareto Analysis of Safety Hazard Escapes (*N* = 300 Adversarial Hazards)

- **`64.4%` (`38 / 59`) of all Best-Practices safety escapes come from a single hazard class: Hollow / Tautological Tests** (test suites that pass `npm test` and look reasonable during static diff review, but fail to kill surviving AST code mutants).
- The remaining **`35.6%` (`21 / 59`)** come from **Frozen Rail Tampering (`18.6%`)** and **Out-of-Scope File Drift (`16.9%`)**, where a subagent under strike-2 test-failure pressure edits a protected rail or an out-of-scope file and rationalizes the edit in its completion message.
